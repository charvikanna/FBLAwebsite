Lesson 5: Investing Basics

Stocks, bonds, diversification, and compound growth.

1 / 4: Content

Asset Classes

Asset classes are groups of investments with different risk and return behavior. Stocks represent ownership in companies and can grow over time, but prices move up and down. Bonds are loans to governments or companies and often provide steadier income with lower growth potential. Cash is stable and liquid but usually grows more slowly.

A practical way to think about this: your money has jobs. Some money protects short-term needs (cash), some provides balance (bonds), and some is aimed at long-term growth (stocks). The right mix depends on your timeline and comfort with risk.

Career and education connection: your biggest \"investment\" early in life may be education, certifications, and job skills. Comparing the cost of training with expected income impact is a real investing decision.

Diversification & Compound Interest

Diversification means spreading money across different assets so one bad outcome does not damage your entire portfolio. It does not remove all risk, but it reduces concentration risk and can make results more stable over time.

Compound growth means your returns start generating their own returns. For example, if you invest consistently each month, your balance can grow faster in later years because you are earning on both original contributions and prior gains.

Taxes matter in investing too. Gains, dividends, and account type can change how much you keep. Learning basic tax treatment helps you make smarter account and withdrawal choices over time.

Daily-life takeaway: Start with a simple rule: invest regularly, diversify broadly, and avoid emotional decisions based on short-term headlines.
