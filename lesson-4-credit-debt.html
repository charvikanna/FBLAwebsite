Lesson 4: Credit & Debt Management

Credit scores and debt payoff strategies.

1 / 4: Content

Understanding Credit

Your credit score is a snapshot of borrowing reliability, usually on a 300-850 scale. Lenders use it to decide whether to approve you for credit cards, car loans, apartment applications, and sometimes even insurance pricing. Strong credit can lower costs over time.

Major factors include payment history, credit utilization, account age, and account mix. Paying on time is usually the most important behavior. Utilization is the percentage of your credit limit you are using; lower utilization generally signals lower risk.

Example: if your credit card limit is $1,000 and your balance is $250, your utilization is 25%. Keeping utilization low and paying on time each month builds trust with lenders and protects your future options.

Scams and fraud connection: Credit safety includes monitoring accounts for suspicious activity, enabling account alerts, using strong passwords, and checking credit reports regularly for identity theft warning signs.

Paying Down Debt

Debt payoff strategy matters because interest can grow quickly. With the Avalanche method, you pay minimums on all debts and put extra money toward the highest interest rate first. With the Snowball method, you pay smallest balances first to build momentum.

Neither method is \"wrong\". Avalanche is often cheaper mathematically, while Snowball can be easier psychologically. Choose the method you are most likely to stick with, then automate payments so progress continues even during busy weeks.

This lesson also applies to student loans and personal loans: understand APR, repayment term, grace periods, and total repayment amount, not just monthly payment size.

Daily-life takeaway: Make at least one debt decision rule: \"No new debt unless I can explain the total cost and repayment plan in one sentence.\"
