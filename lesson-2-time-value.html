Lesson 2: Time Value of Money (TVM)

PV, FV, annuities, and discount rates.

1 / 4: Content

Definition

The time value of money (TVM), also called present discounted value (PDV), is the financial principle that a dollar today is more valuable than that same dollar in the future because today’s dollar can be invested to earn returns over time.

TVM is about timing + growth: the amount matters, but when you receive or spend that amount matters just as much.

What Is TVM?

TVM explains how cash grows or shrinks in real value over time. In practice, finance teams use TVM to compare alternatives, such as receiving cash now versus later, evaluating investment opportunities, and pricing loans.

Present Value (PV)

What future money is worth today after discounting for time and required return.

Future Value (FV)

What today’s money can grow into after compounding at a given rate.

Rate of Return (i)

The annual growth or discount rate used to move money forward or backward in time.

Time + Compounding

More years and more frequent compounding usually increase future value.

Key note: TVM models growth using assumptions. If returns are negative or capital is lost, the result changes accordingly.

Power of Compound Interest

Compound interest means returns are earned on both the original principal and prior returns. This creates an accelerating effect over longer time periods.

At the same time, inflation reduces purchasing power. If your money does not earn at least enough to keep up with inflation, its real value falls even when the dollar amount stays the same.

Time Value of Money Formula

The standard future value formula is:

FV = PV x (1 + i / n)n x t

Use this to project how much a present amount can grow by a future date.

  • FV = future value
  • PV = present value
  • i = annual interest rate
  • n = compounding periods per year
  • t = number of years

To discount future cash into today’s dollars, rearrange to present value form: PV = FV / (1 + i / n)n x t.

Worked Examples

Example A: Future Value

Invest $10,000 for 1 year at 10% compounded annually.

FV = 10,000 x (1 + 0.10/1)1 x 1 = 11,000

Result: End value is $11,000.

Example B: Present Value

Find today’s value of $5,000 received in 1 year at 7%.

PV = 5,000 / (1 + 0.07/1)1 x 1 = 4,673

Result: Today’s equivalent is about $4,673.

Effect of Compounding Frequency

Using $10,000 at 10% for 1 year:

  • Quarterly compounding: $11,038
  • Monthly compounding: $11,047
  • Daily compounding: $11,052

More frequent compounding increases future value because returns are being added to principal more often.

TVM and Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost is the return you give up by not using money in the best available alternative. In TVM terms, accepting money later instead of now can mean losing possible investment gains in the meantime.

Example: if cash today could earn 8% annually, choosing to wait for the same nominal amount later creates a hidden cost equal to that forgone growth.

Why TVM Matters in Finance

  • Compares projects with different payout timing
  • Supports DCF (discounted cash flow) valuation
  • Guides budgeting, borrowing, and investing decisions
  • Used in pension planning and risk management

Bottom line: TVM helps you make better decisions by recognizing that timing is as important as amount.
