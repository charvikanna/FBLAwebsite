Introduction to Financial Statements

Understanding the three core financial statements every investor and business student must know.

1. Income Statement

The Income Statement (also called P&L—Profit and Loss) shows a company's financial performance over a period (e.g., quarter or year). It flows from top to bottom:

  • Revenue – Total sales or income
  • Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) – Direct costs of producing goods
  • Gross Profit = Revenue − COGS
  • Operating Expenses – Rent, salaries, marketing, etc.
  • Operating Income (EBIT) = Gross Profit − Operating Expenses
  • Net Income = Operating Income − Interest − Taxes

2. Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet is a snapshot at a point in time. The fundamental equation:

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

  • Assets – What the company owns (cash, inventory, property, receivables)
  • Liabilities – What it owes (debt, payables)
  • Equity – Owners' stake (net worth)

3. Cash Flow Statement

The Cash Flow Statement shows how cash moves in and out. Three sections:

  • Operating – Cash from day-to-day business
  • Investing – CapEx, acquisitions, asset sales
  • Financing – Debt, equity, dividends

Source: Concepts based on SEC Investor.gov and CFA Institute curriculum.
